B0.05 — Recommended

This number of beds is chosen so that, on an average day, there is no more than a 5% chance that demand will rise above the target average utilization rate. With an 85% utilization target, it means each site should stay at or below 85% full most of the time, while still leaving some beds available for busier days.

Balanced planning means choosing enough beds so each site can usually stay at or below the target average utilization, while still keeping some beds free for busier days.

Baverage
Baverage

This number of beds is calculated from the average number of beds usually needed, then adds a small safety cushion for normal fluctuations. For example, if a site usually needs 20 beds, Baverage adds a few extra beds, but it does not set a specific limit on the chance of exceeding capacity during unusually busy periods.

—

Fewer beds for usual days. Easier to staff and use, but busy days may run short.

B0.01
B0.01

This number of beds is chosen so that, on an average day, there is no more than a 1% chance that demand will rise above the target average utilization rate. With an 85% utilization target, it means each site should stay at or below 85% full even more often than under B0.05, although more beds may remain unused on typical days.

—

More beds for extra protection. Safer for surges, but some beds may stay empty.

Bmax
Bmax

This number of beds is calculated from the highest expected occupancy and then adds a safety cushion; Bmax refers to the maximum expected occupancy, not the largest capacity recommendation. For example, if the busiest expected day requires 30 occupied beds, Bmax adds a few more beds for that peak, but B0.05 or B0.01 may still recommend more because they use explicit utilization-rate and overflow-risk targets.

—

Planning for the busiest predicted days. Strong cushion, but more unused capacity.
