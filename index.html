B0.05 — Recommended

This number of beds is chosen so that, on an average day, there is no more than a 5% chance that demand will rise above the target average utilization. With the default 85% target, it means each site should stay at or below 85% full most of the time, while still leaving some beds available for busier days. It provides a balance between having enough protection and avoiding too many empty beds.

Balanced planning means choosing enough beds so each site can usually stay at or below the target average utilization, while still keeping some beds free for busier days.

Baverage
Baverage

This number is based mainly on how many beds are usually needed, with a small extra cushion added. It works well when demand stays close to normal. The benefit is that fewer beds are needed, making them easier to staff and use efficiently. The downside is that there may not be enough beds during unusually busy periods because this strategy does not set a specific overflow-risk limit.

—

Fewer beds for usual days. Easier to staff and use, but busy days may run short.

B0.01
B0.01

This number of beds is chosen so that there is no more than a 1% chance that demand will rise above the target average utilization. It gives stronger protection than B0.05 and leaves more room for unexpected surges. The trade-off is that more beds must be funded and staffed, and some may often remain unused.

—

More beds for extra protection. Safer for surges, but some beds may stay empty.

Bmax
Bmax

This number starts with the busiest day the model expects and then adds an extra safety cushion. Its benefit is that each site is better prepared for the highest predicted demand. However, it does not promise a specific 5% or 1% overflow-risk target, and the extra beds may be unused during normal periods.

—

Most extra beds for the busiest predicted days. Strong cushion, but more unused capacity.
